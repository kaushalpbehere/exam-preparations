ITIL Specialty Certificate (e.g., BRM / High-Velocity IT)

LAST-MINUTE CRAM (SPECIALTY): Focus deeply on specialized practices like Business Relationship Management (BRM), High-Velocity IT ethics, and complex Cloud Provider integrations.

Set 3 (Questions 101-150)

Q101. What is the core focus of 'Business Empathy' within the BRM practice? (A) Feeling sorry for the customer (B) The ability to deeply understand and share the feelings, perspectives, and business challenges of the customer to build trust and design better solutions (C) Offering discounts (D) Avoiding conflict
Answer: B - Explanation: Empathy is a highly strategic skill for BRMs. If they don't intimately understand the customer's pain, they cannot advocate for the right IT solutions.
Q102. In a digital transformation, what is a 'Value Proposition'? (A) A list of software features (B) A clear, compelling statement that explains how a specific product or service solves a customer's problem or improves their situation, delivering specific benefits (C) A pricing model (D) A legal contract
Answer: B - Explanation: The IT department must define clear value propositions for its services, rather than just offering 'server hosting'.
Q103. What is the difference between 'Customer' and 'User' in ITIL? (A) They are exactly the same (B) A Customer defines the requirements for a service and takes responsibility for the outcomes of service consumption (pays the bill). A User is the person who actually uses the service day-to-day (C) Customers are internal; users are external (D) Users pay for the service
Answer: B - Explanation: The HR Director is the Customer who buys the new Payroll software. The HR clerk who enters data every day is the User. You must satisfy both.
Q104. What is 'Demand Management'? (A) Forcing users to buy things (B) Understanding, anticipating, and influencing customer demand for services to ensure the service provider has the right capacity to meet it (e.g., offering off-peak discounts to shift load) (C) Ignoring customer requests (D) Buying servers in advance
Answer: B - Explanation: You can't just react to demand. You must shape it proactively so IT isn't overwhelmed during peak periods.
Q105. How does 'Portfolio Management' differ from 'Project Management'? (A) They are the same (B) Project Management ensures a specific project is delivered 'right' (on time, on budget). Portfolio Management ensures the organization is doing the 'right projects' (ones that align with strategy and ROI) (C) Portfolio is only for finance (D) Project management is higher level
Answer: B - Explanation: A perfectly executed project that delivers a product nobody wants is a failure of Portfolio Management.
Q106. What is the role of a 'Sponsor' in a strategic IT initiative? (A) The person writing the code (B) The senior executive who authorizes the budget, champions the initiative, and ensures organizational alignment and resources are available (C) The person who tests the software (D) An external vendor
Answer: B - Explanation: Without strong, engaged executive sponsorship, large-scale digital transformations almost always fail due to organizational resistance.
Q107. What is 'Customer Experience' (CX)? (A) The UI of a single app (B) The sum of all interactions a customer has with an organization over the entire lifecycle of their relationship, encompassing emotional, physical, and psychological aspects (C) How fast the website loads (D) Customer support only
Answer: B - Explanation: CX is holistic. If the app is brilliant but the billing department is rude, the overall CX is terrible.
Q108. What is 'User Experience' (UX)? (A) The same as CX (B) A subset of CX focusing specifically on the usability, accessibility, and pleasure provided in the interaction with a specific digital product or interface (C) Hardware design (D) Financial cost
Answer: B - Explanation: UX focuses closely on the actual tool (e.g., is the checkout button easy to find?).
Q109. What does the 'Voice of the Customer' (VoC) refer to? (A) The customer shouting (B) The in-depth process of capturing customer's expectations, preferences, and aversions through surveys, interviews, and behavioral data to inform service design (C) The hold music on the service desk (D) Marketing slogans
Answer: B - Explanation: VoC programs ensure that Service Management decisions are driven by actual customer data, not IT's assumptions of what the customer wants.
Q110. In BRM, what is 'Relationship Mapping'? (A) Dating advice (B) Visually mapping the key stakeholders within the customer organization, identifying their influence, interest levels, and interdependencies to tailor communication strategies (C) Mapping network cables (D) Creating organizational charts
Answer: B - Explanation: BRMs must know who the real decision-makers are and who influences them to navigate complex corporate politics.
Q111. What is the 'Kano Model' used for in service design? (A) Designing databases (B) A theory of product development that classifies customer preferences into five categories (e.g., Must-be, Performance, Attractive) to prioritize feature development based on customer satisfaction impact (C) Calculating server costs (D) Building physical hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: It helps differentiate between basic expectations (Must-be: the car needs brakes) and 'delighters' (Attractive: the car has a built-in espresso machine). Delighters eventually become basic expectations over time.
Q112. What is a 'Service Level Agreement' (SLA) from a strategic BRM perspective? (A) A weapon to punish IT (B) A documented agreement between a service provider and a customer that identifies both services required and the expected level of service, serving as a tool for alignment and realistic expectation setting (C) A technical server manual (D) An employment contract
Answer: B - Explanation: BRMs use SLAs not as strict legal constraints, but as frameworks to ensure IT and the Business agree on what 'good' looks like.
Q113. Explain 'Graceful Degradation' in High-Velocity systems. (A) Deleting old data (B) Designing a system so that when a component fails, the system continues to operate, perhaps with reduced functionality, rather than crashing completely (C) Firing employees slowly (D) Downgrading server hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: If the recommendation engine on Netflix goes down, you shouldn't get a 504 server error. The UI should gracefully hide that section and let you continue to search and watch videos.
Q114. What is 'Circuit Breaker' pattern in microservices? (A) An electrical switch (B) A software design pattern that monitors for widespread failures in a downstream service. If failure rates spike, the circuit 'trips,' instantly blocking requests to give the failing service time to recover, rather than overwhelming it (C) A firewall rule (D) A physical router
Answer: B - Explanation: Without circuit breakers, a slow database can cause every upstream microservice requesting data to hang, bringing down the entire application cascade.
Q115. What is the 'Blast Radius' in incident management? (A) Physical explosion size (B) The extent of the impact of a failure or a change. High-Velocity IT uses techniques like canary releases to intentionally keep the blast radius of new code as small as possible (C) The number of emails sent (D) The size of the IT budget
Answer: B - Explanation: You can never stop 100% of bugs. The goal is to ensure that when a bug hits production, it only impacts 1% of users (small blast radius), not 100%.
Q116. What does 'Mean Time To Recover' (MTTR) measure? (A) How long it takes developers to write code (B) The average time it takes to fully restore a service after a failure occurs. High-Velocity IT focuses heavily on reducing MTTR rather than trying to infinitely increase Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF) (C) The time to hire a new employee (D) Server uptime
Answer: B - Explanation: Embracing failure means shifting focus. It's better to fail often but recover in 5 seconds (low MTTR) than fail rarely but take 3 days to fix it.
Q117. What is 'Telemetry' in software systems? (A) Remote controlling a robot (B) The automated, continuous collection and transmission of data (metrics, logs, traces) from remote systems to a centralized monitoring system to enable Observability (C) Watching TV (D) Sending emails
Answer: B - Explanation: Telemetry is the instrumentation of the code. Without it, you are flying blind in a microservice architecture.
Q118. What is an 'API Gateway'? (A) A physical door lock (B) A server that acts as an API front-end, receiving API requests, enforcing throttling and security policies, passing requests to the back-end service, and then passing the response back to the requester (C) A programming language (D) A type of database
Answer: B - Explanation: It separates the internal microservices from the public internet, centralizing security, routing, and rate-limiting.
Q119. What is 'Rate Limiting' (or Throttling)? (A) Making servers run slower intentionally (B) Controlling the rate of traffic sent or received by a network to prevent a service from being overwhelmed by a sudden spike in legitimate traffic or a Denial of Service attack (C) Limiting developer salaries (D) Slowing down project delivery
Answer: B - Explanation: An API Gateway uses rate limiting to ensure that one overly aggressive client doesn't consume 100% of the server's CPU, starving out all other users.
Q120. In High-Velocity IT, what is the role of 'Internal Developer Platforms' (IDPs)? (A) Storing company policies (B) Providing developers with self-service tools, templates, and automated infrastructure provisioning, allowing them to build and deploy without waiting for Ops teams, reducing cognitive load (C) A social media network for coders (D) A training manual
Answer: B - Explanation: IDPs abstract away the complexity of Kubernetes and AWS. Developers just push their code, and the IDP handles the complex deployment plumbing securely.
Q121. What is 'Cognitive Load' in the context of developer productivity? (A) How smart a developer is (B) The total amount of mental effort being used in the working memory. High-Velocity IT aims to reduce cognitive load by abstracting infrastructure complexity so developers can focus solely on business logic (C) The size of a database (D) The number of meetings per week
Answer: B - Explanation: If a developer has to spend 4 hours configuring firewall rules and AWS IAM permissions before writing a line of code, cognitive load is too high.
Q122. Define 'Value Stream Management' (VSM). (A) Managing money (B) A leadership and technical discipline that enables maximizing flow of business value from idea to customer delivery by connecting and visualizing disparate DevOps tools and data (C) Creating organizational silos (D) Managing a river
Answer: B - Explanation: VSM elevates DevOps from just 'fast technical delivery' to 'delivering the right business value fast'.
Q123. What is a 'Hackathon' often used for in corporate cultures? (A) Stealing competitor data (B) A structured event where cross-functional teams collaborate intensively over a short period (e.g., 24-48 hours) to rapidly prototype innovative solutions to specific problems (C) Breaking into mainframes (D) Writing documentation
Answer: B - Explanation: Hackathons foster innovation, break down silos, and provide a safe space for high-velocity experimentation.
Q124. How does 'Continual Improvement' relate to Agile 'Retrospectives'? (A) They are opposite concepts (B) They are highly aligned; retrospectives are a formal cadence within the lifecycle to identify what went well, what failed, and immediately apply those lessons to the very next iteration (C) Retrospectives are a waste of time (D) Continual improvement is only for hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: Agile embeds continual improvement deeply into the methodology. You don't wait a year to improve; you improve every 2 weeks.
Q125. What is 'Design for Operations'? (A) Building beautiful UI (B) The practice of ensuring that non-functional requirements (like maintainability, observability, and operability) are built into the software from day one, rather than treated as an afterthought (C) Ignoring developers (D) Buying expensive servers
Answer: B - Explanation: If you design a brilliant system that is impossible to monitor or update safely without downtime, it is a failed design.
Q126. What does the term 'Bimodal IT' refer to, and how is it currently viewed? (A) Using two monitors (B) An older concept of splitting IT into Mode 1 (slow, stable legacy) and Mode 2 (fast, agile digital). It is now largely criticized because it creates deeper silos and starves the 'legacy' systems of necessary modernization (C) Using Mac and PC (D) Using two cloud providers
Answer: B - Explanation: ITIL v5 and modern DevOps prefer applying agile principles holistically to all systems, rather than creating a two-class corporate structure.
Q127. In 'Capacity Management' for the Cloud, how does the approach shift? (A) It becomes solely about negotiating bulk discounts on physical servers (B) It shifts from long-term capital expenditure (CapEx) planning for hardware, to real-time operational expenditure (OpEx) automated scaling and FinOps constraints (C) It is no longer needed (D) It requires longer planning cycles
Answer: B - Explanation: You no longer predict capacity 3 years out. You write auto-scaling rules that add server instances automatically when CPU hits 80%, and tear them down when demand drops.
Q128. What is a 'Service Level Pattern' (often called an 'Anti-pattern') to avoid? (A) Measuring outcomes (B) Defining SLAs that are overly complex, technically focused, and entirely disconnected from the actual business value being delivered (C) Communicating with users (D) Tracking tickets
Answer: B - Explanation: Good SLAs/XLAs are simple, understandable by the business sponsor, and directly tied to the desired outcome.
Q129. In a modern, complex multi-supplier environment, who ultimately retains the accountability for the overall service delivered to the customer? (A) The Cloud Provider (AWS/Azure) (B) The specific vendor who caused the outage (C) The enterprise Service Provider acting as the Service Broker integrating all the downstream suppliers (D) The end-user
Answer: C - Explanation: You can outsource the execution and the responsibility to a vendor, but you cannot outsource your ultimate accountability to your customer. If AWS goes down, your customer blames YOU, not AWS.
Q130. What is 'Data Governance'? (A) Deleting old data (B) The overall management of the availability, usability, integrity, and security of data used in an enterprise, ensuring it is treated as a strategic asset (C) Buying hard drives (D) Printing reports
Answer: B - Explanation: High-velocity analytics and AI require clean, trusted data. Data governance ensures the data feeding the AI is accurate and compliant.
Q131. How does 'Data Privacy' (e.g., GDPR) impact High-Velocity IT? (A) It prevents speed entirely (B) It requires 'Privacy by Design', meaning privacy controls must be architected into the system from the beginning, not bolted on as an afterthought, to allow rapid deployment without violating laws (C) It only applies to European companies (D) It ignores cloud computing
Answer: B - Explanation: Re-engineering a live system for GDPR compliance is massively expensive. High velocity requires compliance to be automated via the CI/CD pipeline.
Q132. What is 'Digital Transformation' fundamentally about? (A) Buying iPads for everyone (B) Rethinking the organization's business model, customer experience, and underlying operational processes, heavily leveraging digital technologies as the core enabler (C) Upgrading Windows (D) Moving to a new office
Answer: B - Explanation: It's a business transformation enabled by tech, not just an IT project. Digitizing a bad process just gives you a fast bad process.
Q133. What is the role of a 'Product Owner' in agile service delivery? (A) Writing code (B) Maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the development team, primarily by managing and prioritizing the Product Backlog based on customer needs (C) Firing staff (D) Securing funding
Answer: B - Explanation: The Product Owner acts as the voice of the customer within the engineering team, ensuring they are building the right features.
Q134. How does 'Agile' differ from 'Waterfall'? (A) Agile is slower (B) Waterfall attempts to perfectly design and plan the entire project upfront (predictive). Agile assumes requirements will change and delivers work in small, iterative increments (empirical) to allow for rapid adaptation (C) There is no difference (D) Waterfall is for software, Agile is for hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: Waterfall fails in complex environments because you cannot predict the future. Agile uses fast feedback loops to steer continuously.
Q135. What is 'Scrum'? (A) A rugby formation (B) A specific, lightweight agile framework for developing, delivering, and sustaining complex products, utilizing specific roles (Scrum Master, Product Owner), events (Sprints), and artifacts (Backlogs) (C) A database type (D) A programming language
Answer: B - Explanation: Scrum is the most popular framework for implementing Agile principles in software development teams.
Q136. What is the purpose of a 'Sprint Retrospective' in Scrum? (A) Blaming the team for missing a deadline (B) A dedicated event at the end of a sprint for the team to inspect how the sprint went regards to individuals, interactions, processes, and tools, and to plan improvements for the next sprint (C) Presenting work to the customer (D) Writing code
Answer: B - Explanation: This is the core engine of Continual Improvement in Agile. It creates a formal cadence for reviewing and optimizing the team's processes.
Q137. Difference between 'Kanban' and 'Scrum'? (A) They are the same (B) Scrum uses fixed-length, timeboxed iterations (Sprints). Kanban uses continuous flow, focuses heavily on visualizing work, and strictly relies on WIP (Work In Progress) limits to manage capacity without timeboxes (C) Scrum is for manufacturing (D) Kanban requires a Scrum Master
Answer: B - Explanation: Many teams blend them (Scrumban). Kanban is often better for Operations/Support teams where work (incidents) is highly unpredictable and cannot be planned in 2-week sprints.
Q138. What is 'Servant Leadership'? (A) Making the team fetch coffee (B) A leadership philosophy where the main goal of the leader is to serve the team—removing impediments, fostering psychological safety, and empowering them to make decisions, rather than commanding them (C) Top-down dictatorial management (D) Hiring outside consultants
Answer: B - Explanation: Agile and High-Velocity IT require empowered teams. Servant leaders facilitate that empowerment rather than acting as a bottleneck for decisions.
Q139. What is 'Event-Driven Architecture'? (A) Planning parties (B) A software architecture paradigm promoting the production, detection, consumption of, and reaction to events (state changes) in real-time, heavily used in microservices to ensure loose coupling (C) Monolithic coding (D) Scheduled batch processing
Answer: B - Explanation: Instead of Microservice A asking Microservice B for data (which tightly couples them). Microservice B broadcasts an 'Item Purchased' event asynchronously, and any service that cares listens for it.
Q140. What is 'Serverless Computing' (e.g., AWS Lambda)? (A) Running code without computers (B) A cloud-computing execution model where the cloud provider dynamically manages the allocation and provisioning of servers; the developer only writes the code (functions) and pays precisely for the milliseconds of compute time used (C) Buying on-premise servers (D) Using desktop computers only
Answer: B - Explanation: Serverless abstracts away infrastructure entirely. It allows extreme velocity because developers never have to patch an OS or worry about scaling a server.
Q141. What is the 'Strangler Fig' pattern? (A) A method for killing legacy systems (B) Incrementally migrating a legacy monolithic system by gradually replacing specific pieces of functionality with new microservices, continuing until the old system can be entirely decommissioned (C) A security attack (D) A networking loop
Answer: B - Explanation: Big-bang rewrites of massive legacy systems are extremely risky and often fail. The strangler pattern reduces risk by migrating piece by piece.
Q142. What does 'API First' design signify? (A) Writing the API documentation last (B) Developing the Application Programming Interface (API) collaboratively and explicitly before writing the underlying application code, ensuring consistent, reusable, and predictable integration points (C) Ignoring UI completely (D) Using only REST
Answer: B - Explanation: If you design a microservice without designing the API first, you will likely build something that other teams find difficult or impossible to consume.
Q143. What is 'GitOps'? (A) Using Git for HR documents (B) Applying DevOps best practices (version control, compliance, CI/CD) to infrastructure automation. Git becomes the single source of truth for the declarative infrastructure, and any changes to Git automatically trigger infrastructure updates (C) Using Git without branching (D) A specific Git hosting provider
Answer: B - Explanation: GitOps takes Infrastructure as Code to the next level. If someone manually changes a firewall rule, the GitOps agent notices the drift and immediately changes it back to match the Git repository.
Q144. What is 'Progressive Delivery'? (A) Waterfall methodology (B) An umbrella term for modern deployment techniques like Canary Releases, Feature Flags, and Blue/Green deployments that decouple deployment from release and limit the blast radius of new features (C) Delivering hardware slowly (D) A courier service
Answer: B - Explanation: Progressive delivery shifts the focus from 'deploying code' to 'safely releasing value to users'.
Q145. What is a 'Feature Flag' (or Feature Toggle)? (A) A physical flag on a server (B) A software engineering technique that allows teams to turn specific features of an application on or off during runtime without deploying new code, useful for dark launching or fast rollbacks (C) A bug tracking status (D) A compiler setting
Answer: B - Explanation: Feature flags are crucial for Continuous Deployment. If a new feature causes the database to spike, you don't roll back the whole deployment; you just flip the feature flag to 'off' in milliseconds.
Q146. Tell me about 'Behavior-Driven Development' (BDD). (A) Punishing bad behavior (B) An agile software development process that encourages collaboration among developers, QA, and non-technical or business participants in a software project, defining test cases in plain 'Given/When/Then' language (C) A type of database (D) A performance management review
Answer: B - Explanation: BDD ensures the business and the developers share perfectly aligned expectations of exactly what the software should do before code is written.
Q147. What is 'Test-Driven Development' (TDD)? (A) Testing after the product ships (B) A software development process relying on a very short development cycle: requirements are turned into specific test cases, then the software is improved so that the tests pass. (Red-Green-Refactor) (C) Hiring testers to manage developers (D) Manual UI testing only
Answer: B - Explanation: TDD guarantees high test coverage and forces developers to think about how code will be consumed before they write the implementation.
Q148. What is 'Continuous Testing'? (A) Testing 24 hours a day manually (B) The process of executing automated tests as part of the software delivery pipeline to obtain immediate feedback on the business risks associated with a software release candidate (C) Using cheap offshore testers (D) Ignoring production errors
Answer: B - Explanation: You cannot achieve Continuous Delivery without Continuous Testing. The pipeline itself must become the definitive quality gate.
Q149. In terms of resilience, what does 'Redundancy' mean? (A) Firing staff (B) The duplication of critical components or functions of a system with the intention of increasing reliability of the system, usually in the form of a backup or fail-safe (C) Writing the same code twice (D) Having two project managers
Answer: B - Explanation: If a critical database has no redundancy and the hard drive physically fails, the service is destroyed. Redundant fail-overs handle these hardware failures seamlessly.
Q150. Final Specialty Question: What is the primary difference between a 'Disaster Recovery Plan' (DRP) and a 'Business Continuity Plan' (BCP)? (A) They are identical (B) DRP is specifically focused on recovering the IT infrastructure (servers, data) after a catastrophic event. BCP is the broader organizational plan to keep the entire business operations functioning during that disaster (moving staff to a new building, manual workarounds) (C) DRP is for hardware, BCP is for software (D) BCP is only for pandemics
Answer: B - Explanation: If IT perfectly executes the DRP and the servers are restored, it's useless if the BCP hasn't arranged a backup office for the employees to sit at to access them.
