ITIL Specialty Certificate (e.g., BRM / High-Velocity IT)

LAST-MINUTE CRAM (SPECIALTY): Focus deeply on specialized practices like Business Relationship Management (BRM), High-Velocity IT ethics, and complex Cloud Provider integrations.

Set 4 (Questions 151-200)

Q151. What is the defining characteristic of a 'Micro-service' architecture compared to a monolithic one? (A) Microservices run on smaller hardware (B) The application is structured as a collection of loosely coupled, independently deployable services organized around business capabilities (C) Microservices are always written in JavaScript (D) Monoliths are faster to deploy
Answer: B - Explanation: Independence is key. Team A can deploy an update to the 'Search' microservice 5 times a day without coordinating with Team B who manages the 'Checkout' microservice.
Q152. What is the consequence of 'Tight Coupling' in software architecture? (A) Faster deployment (B) Making a change in one component requires changes in other components, increasing the risk of cascading failures and drastically slowing down deployment velocity (C) Better security (D) Easier testing
Answer: B - Explanation: Tight coupling forces teams to coordinate release schedules (a 'Release Train'), which destroys the agility promised by CI/CD pipelines.
Q153. What is typical of a 'Platform Team' in a High-Velocity organization (e.g., Team Topologies)? (A) They write business features (B) They treat the internal developers as their 'customers,' providing a self-service internal platform (IDP) that abstracts away infrastructure complexity and reduces cognitive load (C) They manually build servers upon receiving tickets (D) They do manual QA testing
Answer: B - Explanation: The Platform Team builds the paved road. Developers shouldn't have to learn the intricacies of Kubernetes networking; the Platform Team automates it for them.
Q154. What constitutes 'Enabling Teams' in the Team Topologies model? (A) Helpdesk managers (B) Specialized teams composed of experts in a specific domain (e.g., Security, UI testing) who temporarily assist Stream-aligned teams to overcome technical hurdles and upskill them, rather than doing the work for them (C) External consultants (D) Hardware repair technicians
Answer: B - Explanation: Enabling teams act as coaches. They teach the delivery team how to fish, rather than handing them a fish, preventing bottlenecks.
Q155. What is the primary role of 'Stream-aligned Teams'? (A) Managing cloud billing (B) Empowered, cross-functional teams aligned to a single, continuous flow of work (a product, service, or user journey), capable of delivering value end-to-end without waiting on external handoffs (C) Building physical network cables (D) Managing HR processes
Answer: B - Explanation: Handoffs are the enemy of speed. A stream-aligned team has a front-end dev, backend dev, tester, and ops person all on the same team.
Q156. In SRE, what happens when an 'Error Budget' is exhausted? (A) The team gets a financial bonus (B) The team halts or slows down feature deployments and redirects all engineering effort toward improving system reliability and paying down technical debt until the budget recovers (C) The team ignores the budget (D) The Service Desk takes over development
Answer: B - Explanation: This provides a mathematical, objective way to balance the friction between Product Owners (who want features) and Operations (who want stability).
Q157. What is the purpose of 'Chaos Monkey' (developed by Netflix)? (A) A team-building game (B) A tool that randomly terminates virtual machine instances and containers in the production environment to enforce the principle that engineers must build resilient architectures that survive unexpected outages (C) A monitoring tool (D) A bug tracking system
Answer: B - Explanation: If you know a monkey is running through your server room pulling out cables, you build a system where pulling one cable doesn't bring down the whole app.
Q158. How does 'Observability' complement 'Monitoring'? (A) They are identical (B) Monitoring dashboards show that metrics (CPU/Memory) are red. Observability provides the deep telemetry (Logs, Metrics, Traces) required to dive in and figure out *why* they are red, especially for novel, unpredicted failures (C) Monitoring is for hardware, Observability is for software (D) Observability is a manual process
Answer: B - Explanation: Monitoring answers 'Is it broken?'. Observability answers 'Why is it broken, and where exactly is the fault?'.
Q159. What does 'Distributed Tracing' track? (A) Physical delivery trucks (B) A single user request (like clicking 'Buy') as it travels across dozens or hundreds of different microservices, allowing engineers to pinpoint exactly which service caused a delay or failure (C) Network cabling faults (D) Employee attendance
Answer: B - Explanation: In a monolith, you just look at one log file. In microservices, a request hits 20 servers. A trace ID follows that transaction across all 20 servers.
Q160. What is 'Lean UX'? (A) Creating massive design documents (B) Applying lean principles to design, focusing on rapid, iterative creation of 'Minimum Viable Products' to test riskiest assumptions with real users quickly, rather than aiming for perfection (C) Ignoring user interface design (D) Only designing for mobile
Answer: B - Explanation: Don't spend 6 months designing a perfect UI. Sketch it in a week, show it to users, realize it's confusing, and redesign it. Fail fast.
Q161. In the context of Digital Transformation, what is an 'Innovation Sandbox'? (A) A toy for employees (B) A securely isolated cloud environment where teams can rapidly experiment with new tools, APIs, or architectural patterns without risking the production data or systems (C) A meeting room (D) A budget line item
Answer: B - Explanation: You cannot achieve high velocity if developers have to wait 3 weeks for security approval just to test a new open-source library.
Q162. Describe the 'Pioneer, Settler, Town Planner' model by Simon Wardley. (A) It refers to geographical expansion (B) A way to organize teams based on the lifecycle of a technology. Pioneers innovate wildly (high risk/fast), Settlers productize the innovation (scale it), and Town Planners turn it into an efficient, highly reliable utility (C) It is a project management tool (D) It categorizes software licenses
Answer: B - Explanation: You shouldn't put a 'Town Planner' (someone focused on 99.999% uptime and Six Sigma efficiency) in charge of the 'Pioneer' R&D lab.
Q163. What is the 'Cynefin Framework'? (A) A type of database (B) A sense-making framework that helps leaders determine the prevailing context (Clear, Complicated, Complex, or Chaotic) so they can choose the appropriate management response (C) A software testing tool (D) A firewall configuration
Answer: B - Explanation: Applying 'Clear' (Best Practice) solutions to a 'Complex' problem guarantees failure. You must Probe (experiment), Sense, and Respond in complex environments.
Q164. According to Cynefin, how should you manage a 'Chaotic' situation (e.g., a massive cyberattack)? (A) Form a committee to study it (B) Act immediately to establish order, Sense where stability is present, and then Respond to move the situation from Chaotic into Complex (C) Consult best practice manuals (D) Run multiple long-term experiments
Answer: B - Explanation: In chaos, there is no time to analyze. You act decisively (pull the plug on the server) to stop the bleeding, then analyze.
Q165. What does the term 'Systems Thinking' emphasize? (A) Only focusing on hardware (B) Viewing the organization as a holistic, interconnected whole rather than a collection of isolated silos, recognizing that a local optimization in one department might degrade the performance of the entire system (C) Managing local databases (D) Writing isolated code functions
Answer: B - Explanation: If developers optimize to push 100 updates a day, but QA can only test 5, the developers' local optimization just created a massive, blocking queue for the system.
Q166. What is a 'Value Stream'? (A) A financial report (B) The series of steps that an organization undertakes to create and deliver products and services to consumers. It encompasses the end-to-end flow from demand to value delivery (C) A stream of code updates (D) A physical supply chain for hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: A 'Procure to Pay' value stream involves the requester, procurement, the vendor, receiving, and accounts payable. It spans the whole company.
Q167. How does ITIL 4 define a 'Practice' (formerly 'Process' in ITIL v3)? (A) It is just a new word for process (B) A set of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective. A Practice includes the 4 Dimensions (People, Tools, Partners, Processes), not just the flowchart (C) It is strictly a human skill (D) It refers only to automation
Answer: B - Explanation: A process flowchart is useless without trained people, the right tools, and supportive partners. A 'Practice' encompasses all of these.
Q168. What is the core difference between 'Agile' and 'Lean'? (A) Agile is for hardware, Lean is for software (B) Agile focuses heavily on iterative delivery, adapting to changing requirements, and human collaboration. Lean focuses relentlessly on systemic flow and the elimination of all forms of waste (C) They are identical (D) Lean is slower
Answer: B - Explanation: They are highly complementary. Agile gives you the engine for fast iteration; Lean clears the track of obstacles so the engine can run fast.
Q169. What is 'Value Demand' vs 'Failure Demand'? (A) Failure demand is cheaper (B) Value demand is work that directly creates value for the customer (building a feature). Failure demand is work caused by a failure to do something right the first time (fixing bugs, handling complaints) (C) Value demand is only financial (D) There is no difference
Answer: B - Explanation: If 60% of IT's time is spent on Failure Demand (fixing broken things), they only have 40% capacity to actually build value.
Q170. What is 'Site Reliability Engineering' (SRE) primarily known for regarding operations? (A) Replacing all humans with robots (B) Treating operations as if it's a software problem. SREs are software engineers tasked with managing operations, focusing on high automation and creating scalable, highly reliable systems (C) Fixing printers (D) Managing HR compliance
Answer: B - Explanation: 'Hope is not a strategy.' SREs use mathematical approaches (SLIs, SLOs, Error Budgets) to guarantee reliability at massive Google-level scale.
Q171. What does the 'Shift-Left' approach seek to achieve regarding Security (DevSecOps)? (A) Moving security to another country (B) Integrating security controls, vulnerability scanning, and compliance checks directly into the developer's IDE and CI/CD pipeline, rather than treating security as a final gatekeeper at the end (C) Ignoring security (D) Doing security audits manually
Answer: B - Explanation: If a developer writes a vulnerable line of code, the automated pipeline should reject it 5 minutes later, not 6 months later during an audit.
Q172. In a continuous delivery environment, what is a 'Pipeline'? (A) A physical pipe for cooling servers (B) A set of automated processes and tools that developers use to compile, build, test, and deploy code to production in a reliable and repeatable manner (C) A sales funnel (D) A management reporting line
Answer: B - Explanation: A robust pipeline is the heart of DevOps. It ensures that every single code change is subjected to the exact same rigorous testing before it goes live.
Q173. What is the fundamental principle of 'Continuous Improvement'? (A) Making massive changes once a year (B) The ongoing, incremental effort to improve products, services, or processes. It recognizes that perfection is never achieved; there is always a smaller, better, or faster way to do things (C) Only improving when things break (D) Firing the lowest performers annually
Answer: B - Explanation: Rooted in the Japanese concept of Kaizen, the goal is small daily improvements by everyone, everywhere.
Q174. What is the purpose of the 'Continual Improvement Register' (CIR)? (A) A list of employees to fire (B) A database or structured document to track and manage improvement ideas from identification through to final action, ensuring good ideas aren't lost (C) A log of server reboots (D) A financial ledger
Answer: B - Explanation: If you have a retrospective and identify 5 things to fix, but don't record them in a visible CIR, they will be forgotten by tomorrow.
Q175. Why is 'Psychological Safety' fundamental to a blameless culture? (A) It makes work fun (B) Because complex systems fail in complex ways. If employees fear blame or firing, they will hide the systemic flaws, meaning the organization cannot learn, and the same catastrophic failure will happen again (C) It reduces salaries (D) It is an HR mandate
Answer: B - Explanation: The aviation industry is the safest in the world because they deeply investigate every near-miss without blaming the pilot, in order to fix the plane's system.
Q176. Which ITIL practice is responsible for moving new or changed hardware, software, documentation, or other service components to live environments? (A) Release Management (B) Deployment Management (C) Change Enablement (D) Incident Management
Answer: B - Explanation: Deployment is the technical act of moving the code. Release Management is the business act of making it available to the user.
Q177. Which practice manages workarounds and known errors? (A) Incident Management (B) Problem Management (C) Service Request Management (D) Knowledge Management
Answer: B - Explanation: Incident management just wants to get the service running again quickly (using the workaround). Problem Management owns the 'Known Error' record and investigates the root cause.
Q178. What is the primary focus of 'Information Security Management'? (A) Buying antivirus software (B) Understanding and managing risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA) of information, balancing security with the need for usability and agility (C) Locking all server rooms (D) Providing user training
Answer: B - Explanation: Security that is so strict it prevents people from doing their jobs (Zero Usability) will invariably lead to Shadow IT and worse security risks.
Q179. What does 'IT Asset Management' (ITAM) primarily track? (A) Only physical hardware location (B) The full lifecycle, financial cost, and compliance (especially softwarelicensing) of all IT assets, ensuring the organization maximizes value and avoids legal audits (C) Network cabling schemas (D) Employee skill sets
Answer: B - Explanation: A CMDB tracks how Server A talks to Database B (technical). ITAM tracks that Server A costs $500/month and the Database license expires next Tuesday (financial/legal).
Q180. What is a 'Configuration Item' (CI)? (A) A software bug (B) Any component that needs to be managed in order to deliver an IT service (e.g., a server, a software license, a network switch, or even a critical SLA document) (C) An employee (D) A customer request
Answer: B - Explanation: CIs are the building blocks stored in the Configuration Management Database (CMDB).
Q181. What is the purpose of 'Relationship Management' at a strategic level? (A) Taking clients to lunch (B) Establishing and nurturing links between the organization and its stakeholders at strategic and tactical levels, understanding needs, and ensuring value co-creation (C) Managing the Service Desk queues (D) Negotiating hardware prices
Answer: B - Explanation: BRMs live in this practice. They translate business strategy into IT strategy and ensure IT is viewed as a partner, not a cost center.
Q182. How does the 'Guiding Principle: Think and Work Holistically' apply to creating a new mobile app? (A) Only focusing on the app's UI (B) Realizing that the app requires backend servers (Technology), skilled developers (People), third-party authentication (Partners), and an onboarding workflow (Processes). All four dimensions must be considered (C) Only considering the budget (D) Only asking the CEO
Answer: B - Explanation: A fantastic app is useless if the process for getting a login account takes 4 weeks.
Q183. Define a 'Service Offering'. (A) A discount coupon (B) A description of one or more services designed to address the needs of a target consumer group, typically including goods, access to resources, and service actions (C) A technical manual (D) A server specification
Answer: B - Explanation: 'Sales Mobility Package' is a service offering. It includes a physical laptop (Good), VPN access (Resource), and 24/7 dedicated support (Action).
Q184. What is 'Organizational Agility'? (A) Moving desks quickly (B) The ability of an entire organization (not just IT) to move and adapt quickly, flexibly, and decisively to support internal changes and external market shifts (C) Adopting Scrum in one team (D) Writing code faster
Answer: B - Explanation: IT can be perfectly agile, but if corporate finance still requires 18-month budget approvals, the organization lacks agility.
Q185. What does the 'Service Value Chain' activity 'Design and Transition' do? (A) Fixes daily incidents (B) Ensures that products and services continually meet stakeholder expectations for quality, costs, and time to market before they go live (C) Interacts with the end consumer directly (D) Dictates the overall corporate strategy
Answer: B - Explanation: This is the bridge where the raw materials (code, servers) are brought together, tested, and transitioned into the live environment safely.
Q186. In the context of SIAM (Service Integration and Management), what is the 'Retained Organization'? (A) Fired employees (B) The internal governance, architecture, and integration functions that the enterprise must keep in-house, even when outsourcing 90% of the actual technical execution to external suppliers (C) The external vendors (D) The offshore support team
Answer: B - Explanation: You can outsource fixing servers. You cannot outsource strategic business accountability or enterprise architecture.
Q187. What does the term 'Resilience' mean in High-Velocity IT? (A) Never failing (B) The ability of an organization or system to anticipate, prepare for, respond to, and adapt to incremental change and sudden disruptions in order to survive and prosper (C) Having a thick skin (D) Building massive concrete data centers
Answer: B - Explanation: A rigid bridge snaps in a hurricane. A resilient bridge sways. Build systems that bend and recover, because they will inevitably experience stress.
Q188. What is 'Omnichannel' support compared to 'Multichannel'? (A) Omnichannel has fewer channels (B) Multichannel means you have phone and email, but they are siloed. Omnichannel means the channels are deeply integrated; a chat agent can see the email you sent yesterday and the portal articles you just viewed (C) They are exactly the same (D) Omnichannel is only for VIPs
Answer: B - Explanation: Omnichannel provides a seamless customer experience, preserving context regardless of how the user chooses to interact.
Q189. What is the goal of 'Swarming' over traditional Tiered support? (A) Flooding the user with emails (B) Avoiding the massive delay of passing a ticket from Tier 1, to Tier 2, to Tier 3. Instead, bringing the right experts into a shared channel immediately to solve complex novel problems fast (C) Slowing down incident resolution (D) Ignoring SLAs
Answer: B - Explanation: Tiered support is great for standard requests (password resets). It is terrible for complex system outages where no single tier has the whole answer.
Q190. What is 'ChatOps'? (A) Employees gossiping (B) Integrating Chat tools (like Slack) with IT operations tools and bots, allowing teams to execute commands and see alerts collaboratively in a highly visible stream (C) Speaking to customers on the phone (D) Using emojis in code
Answer: B - Explanation: When an incident occurs, a bot posts the monitoring graph in the channel. The engineer types '/restart-server-3' in the chat. Everyone sees the action and the result. Total visibility.
Q191. How does 'Continual Improvement' apply to 'Value Streams'? (A) It doesn't (B) You map the current state of the value stream, identify the biggest bottleneck (waste), execute an improvement initiative to elevate it, and then remap the future state. Repeat forever (C) Value streams never change (D) Only managers can improve them
Answer: B - Explanation: This is the essence of Lean applied to IT. Continually hunting for and destroying waste in the lifecycle.
Q192. What is a 'Sunk Cost Fallacy' in Portfolio Management? (A) A budget surplus (B) Continuing to invest in a failing project simply because you have already spent $1M on it, rather than killing it and reallocating the remaining budget to something valuable (C) Cheaper hardware (D) A successful project
Answer: B - Explanation: High-velocity organizations are ruthless about killing failing experiments. The $1M is gone. Don't waste another $1M out of stubbornness.
Q193. How does 'Zero Trust' differ from traditional network security? (A) It stongly relies on VPNs (B) Traditional security assumes anyone inside the corporate firewall is trusted. Zero Trust assumes the network is already compromised and requires strict authentication and authorization for every single transaction, regardless of location (C) It uses no passwords (D) It has zero security
Answer: B - Explanation: With cloud and remote work, the 'perimeter firewall' model is obsolete. Identity is the new perimeter.
Q194. What does 'Automate everything you can' actually mean in practice? (A) Fire everyone (B) You shouldn't blindly automate. You must first 'Simplify' and 'Optimize' the process to remove waste. Then you automate the remaining necessary steps to eliminate human toil and improve consistency (C) Buy expensive RPA tools (D) Let AI run the company
Answer: B - Explanation: Automating a bad process just generates bad outcomes at machine speed.
Q195. What is the 'Watermelon Effect' regarding SLAs? (A) Excellent user experience (B) All the technical dashboards and SLAs are 'Green' (like the outside of a watermelon), but the actual user experience is terrible and 'Red' (like the inside) (C) A fruit delivery service (D) A seasonal variation in tickets
Answer: B - Explanation: This happens when IT measures things users don't care about (server CPU) instead of measuring actual business outcomes.
Q196. How do 'Experience Level Agreements' (XLAs) solve the Watermelon Effect? (A) By lowering the SLA targets (B) XLAs measure subjective human outcomes and sentiment (e.g., 'Is the application fast enough for you to do your job?') rather than just objective technical metrics (e.g., 'Application latency is <20ms') (C) XLAs only measure executive satisfaction (D) By ignoring metrics
Answer: B - Explanation: A 20ms latency SLA is useless if the UI is so confusing the user can't find the 'Submit' button.
Q197. What is 'AIOps'? (A) Artificial Intelligence Operations (B) The application of machine learning and big data to IT operations problems, specifically used to reduce alert fatigue by correlating massive amounts of logs to pinpoint actual root causes (C) Hiring robots (D) A new programming language
Answer: B - Explanation: Humans cannot read 10,000 log lines a second. AIOps ingests the noise, finds the single anomaly, and proactively alerts the engineer.
Q198. What is 'Design Thinking'? (A) Graphic design (B) A human-centered approach to innovation that draws from the designer's toolkit to integrate the needs of people, the possibilities of technology, and the requirements for business success (C) Writing code without planning (D) Designing databases
Answer: B - Explanation: It involves 5 phases: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test. Empathy is the critical first step.
Q199. What does the principle 'Start Where You Are' require you to do? (A) Throw everything away (B) Perform an objective assessment of the current state, often requiring going to the front lines ('Gemba') to observe how work actually happens, rather than relying on outdated documentation (C) Start coding immediately (D) Move to a new office
Answer: B - Explanation: You cannot plot a route to a destination if you don't accurately know your starting coordinates.
Q200. Final Question: In High-Velocity IT, what is the ultimate arbiter of 'Value'? (A) The IT Manager (B) The Consumer. It doesn't matter how brilliant the architecture is; if the consumer doesn't perceive it as useful or important, it has zero value (C) The Cloud Provider (D) The Service Level Agreement
Answer: B - Explanation: Value is entirely subjective and determined by the receiver. Always focus on outcomes over outputs.
