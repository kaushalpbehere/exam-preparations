ITIL Specialty Certificate - Cram Guide

High-Velocity IT, Business Relationship Management (BRM), and Modern Cloud Operating Models (SRE/DevOps).

Strategic Mindset: Stop thinking about 'closing tickets'. Start thinking about flow, lead time, psychological safety, and viewing IT as an investment portfolio that co-creates business value.

1. High-Velocity IT Core Principles

  • Ethics: Speed is dangerous without ethics. AI bias, data privacy, and societal impact must be governed, not ignored for the sake of velocity.
  • Safety Culture (Psychological Safety): The absolute foundation. If people fear being fired for mistakes, they will hide bugs and stop innovating. Blameless Post-Mortems are mandatory.
  • Lean Thinking: Relentlessly hunt and eliminate 'Waste' (Muda), especially Wait Times, Overproduction, and Toil.
  • Value Co-Creation: IT does not 'deliver' value. Value is generated only when the consumer actively engages with the service to achieve an outcome.

2. Business Relationship Management (BRM)

The BRM is not a helpdesk escalation point. They are deeply strategic.

  • Trusted Advisor: Shaping demand, challenging business assumptions, and aligning the IT portfolio with long-term corporate strategy.
  • Empathy: Deeply understanding the customer's actual pain points, not just their stated technical requirements.
  • Relationship Mapping: Understanding the complex political web of decision-makers and influencers within the enterprise.
  • Voice of the Customer (VoC): Using data and interviews to drive service design based on actual consumer needs.

3. SRE & Modern Operations

Concept Explanation
Toil Manual, repetitive work without enduring value. Must be engineered away so it doesn't scale linearly.
SLI/SLO/SLA SLI: The real-time metric. SLO: The internal engineering target (99.9%). SLA: The external legal contract (99.5%).
Error Budget 100% minus the SLO. It mathematically defines the acceptable risk for deployments. If blown, deployments halt.
Chaos Engineering Intentionally breaking things in production to prove that automated recovery mechanisms actually work.
Observability Unlike monitoring (checking known thresholds), Observability uses logs/traces to debug complex, unknown failures in microservices.

4. Flow, Velocity, and Lean Concepts

  • Theory of Constraints (ToC): Every system has a bottleneck. Improving anything *other* than the bottleneck is a waste of time and only increases Work In Progress (WIP).
  • WIP Limits: "Stop starting, start finishing." Limiting active work prevents context switching and massively improves Lead Time.
  • Lead Time vs Cycle Time: Lead time spans from customer request to final delivery. Cycle time is just the active work time.
  • Value Stream Management (VSM): Visualizing the end-to-end flow of work across silos to eliminate wait times and bottlenecks.

5. Architecture & Deployment Tactics

Deployment ≠ Release: Deployment is moving code to a server. Release is making it available to a user. Decouple them!
  • Microservices: Breaking code into small, independent pieces so teams can deploy without waiting for a massive "Release Train."
  • Strangler Fig Pattern: Safely retiring a legacy monolith by replacing components piecemeal over time.
  • Immutable Infrastructure: Never patch a server. Build a fresh one from code (IaC) to prevent configuration drift.
  • Blue/Green Deployment: Switching 100% of traffic instantly between two identical environments for zero-downtime and instant rollbacks.
  • Canary Release: Routing 5% of traffic to the new version to test for bugs, limiting the 'blast radius'.
  • Feature Flags: Dark launching code that is turned 'off'. It can be flipped 'on' later without a new deployment.

6. Modern Team Topologies

Conway's Law states that organizations design systems mirroring their communication structures. To build microservices, you must first build cross-functional teams.

  • Stream-Aligned Teams: Cross-functional (Dev+Ops+QA) teams aligned to a specific product, capable of end-to-end delivery without handoffs.
  • Platform Teams: They build the "paved road" (Internal Developer Platforms) to abstract away infrastructure complexity and reduce cognitive load for developers.
  • Enabling Teams: Subject matter experts (e.g., Security) who coach and upskill stream-aligned teams, rather than doing the work for them.
