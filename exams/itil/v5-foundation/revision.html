ITIL v5 Foundation - Ultimate Revision Guide

Focus heavily on modern concepts (AIOps, Cloud, Shift-Left, Complexity) integrated into the core ITIL framework.

Key Exam Mindset: Do not think like a traditional ticket-closing technician. Think like a strategic business partner who uses technology, automation, and empathy to rapidly co-create value in an unpredictable, complex world.

1. Core Concepts of Service Management

  • Value: The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. It is always determined by the consumer.
  • Value Co-creation: Value is actively collaborated on between providers and consumers. (A gym membership has no value if you don't use it).
  • Service: A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.
  • Outcome vs Output: An Output is a physical deliverable (a report). An Outcome is the business result achieved using that output (increased sales). Focus on Outcomes!
  • Utility vs Warranty:
    • Utility: Fit for purpose (Does it have the right features?).
    • Warranty: Fit for use (Is it available, secure, performant?).
    • Note: A service must have BOTH to create value.

2. The Four Dimensions of Service Management

To ensure holistic success, organizations must consider all four dimensions simultaneously. Ignoring one leads to failure.

  1. Organizations & People: Roles, skills, culture, psychological safety, breaking down silos, T-shaped professionals.
  2. Information & Technology: The tools, infrastructure, data, AI, cloud computing, and system architectures used.
  3. Partners & Suppliers: Relationships with external vendors (ranging from buying pens to strategic cloud partnerships (Service Broker)).
  4. Value Streams & Processes: How the organization works in an integrated way. Focusing on eliminating waste (Lean).
External Factors (PESTLE): Remember that Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, and Environmental factors constrain all four dimensions.

3. The 7 Guiding Principles

These are universal and enduring recommendations that guide an organization in ALL circumstances.

  • Focus on Value: Everything must map back to creating value for the consumer. User Experience (UX) and Customer Experience (CX) are vital.
  • Start Where You Are: Avoid 'rip and replace'. Objectively measure the current state (Go and See / Gemba) and reuse what works.
  • Progress Iteratively with Feedback: Agile approach. Break work into small chunks, deliver fast, get feedback, and adapt. (MVPs).
  • Collaborate and Promote Visibility: Break down silos. Work in the open (Kanban). Cross-functional teams (Swarming).
  • Think and Work Holistically: Remember the 4 dimensions. Changing software without changing the process will fail.
  • Keep it Simple and Practical: Lean thinking. Eliminate waste. If a step/metric provides no value, stop doing it.
  • Optimize and Automate: ALWAYS optimize (simplify) a process first, THEN automate it to eliminate toil. Do not automate a broken process.

4. The Service Value System (SVS) & Value Chain (SVC)

The SVS represents how all components of an organization work together to turn Demand/Opportunity into Value.

The Service Value Chain (SVC) is the core operating model within the SVS. It has 6 activities:

  1. Plan: Shared understanding of vision, current status, and direction.
  2. Improve: Continual improvement of products and practices.
  3. Engage: The "front door". Understanding stakeholder needs, transparency, relationship building (Service Desk lives here).
  4. Design & Transition: Ensuring products continually meet expectations for quality, cost, and time-to-market.
  5. Obtain/Build: Ensuring service components are available when/where needed (buying software or coding it).
  6. Deliver & Support: Ensuring services are delivered and supported to agreed specifications (Incident Mgmt lives here).

5. Key Modern ITSM Concepts (ITIL v5 Heavy Focus)

Concept Definition / Goal
Complex Systems Modern IT is unpredictable and non-linear. You cannot plan perfectly. You must probe, sense, and respond (Chaos Engineering).
Technical Debt The cost of choosing a fast/hacky solution over doing it right. It must be tracked and continually paid down (refactored).
Toil (SRE) Manual, repetitive work devoid of enduring value. Automation's primary goal is to eliminate toil.
Shift-Left Moving testing, security, and Level 1 support earlier in the pipeline/closer to the user to catch defects when they are cheap to fix.
AIOps & GenAI Using ML to reduce alert noise, predict failures, and draft knowledge base articles automatically.
XLAs (Experience Level Agreements) Moving beyond technical SLAs (uptime) to measuring human outcomes, sentiment, and actual business enablement (The Watermelon Effect).

6. Critical Practices to Know

  • Incident Management: Restoring service as quickly as possible. (Fix what is broken). Uses Swarming for Major Incidents.
  • Problem Management: Finding the root cause to prevent incidents from happening again. Creates Workarounds and Known Errors.
  • Service Request Management: Handling routine, pre-defined, planned requests (Password resets, software installs). Heavy focus on self-service portals and automation.
  • Service Desk: The Single Point of Contact (SPOC). Requires intense empathy and omnichannel support. They are the face of IT.
  • Change Enablement: Maximizing successful changes while managing risk. Standard (Pre-authorized), Normal (CAB/Peer review), Emergency (fix right now).
  • Deployment vs. Release Management:
    • Deployment: Moving code to production (Automated CI/CD, Blue/Green, Canary).
    • Release: Making that code available to the user (Feature Flags / Dark Launching).
  • Continual Improvement: Everyone's responsibility. Uses the Continual Improvement Register (CIR). Aligned heavily with Agile retrospectives.
  • Information Security Management: Protects confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA). Security must be balanced with usability.
  • IT Asset Management (ITAM): Manages the costs, risks, and lifecycle of hardware/software (focussing heavily on license compliance/SAM).
  • Service Configuration Management: Manages the technical relationships and dependencies mapping between CIs in the CMDB.
  • Relationship Management: Strategic level. Understanding stakeholder needs and ensuring a healthy provider-consumer relationship.
