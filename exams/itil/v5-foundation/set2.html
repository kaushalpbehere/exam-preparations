ITIL v5 Foundation

LAST-MINUTE CRAM (ITIL v5): Remember the focus on AI integration, automated service management, value streams, digital transformation, and advanced ITSM capabilities.

Set 2 (Questions 51-100)

Q51. What is the defining characteristic of a 'Complex System' as understood in modern ITIL v5 environments? (A) It is perfectly predictable (B) It is linear and simple to map (C) It exhibits emergent behavior where the whole is greater than the sum of its parts, making prediction and absolute control difficult (D) It never breaks
Answer: C - Explanation: Modern IT environments are complex systems. They are non-linear, highly interconnected, and constantly changing, meaning traditional rigid control mechanisms often fail.
Q52. How should organizations manage change in a Complex System according to ITIL v5? (A) Enforce massive, infrequent, highly risky deployments (B) Through small, safe-to-fail experiments and iterative improvements (C) Ban all changes (D) Only allow management to make changes
Answer: B - Explanation: Because complex systems are unpredictable, the safest way to improve them is through iterative, small-scale changes that can be easily rolled back if they fail.
Q53. What is 'Chaos Engineering'? (A) Firing employees randomly (B) The discipline of experimenting on a system in order to build confidence in the system's capability to withstand turbulent conditions in production (C) Writing bad code on purpose (D) Unmanaged IT services
Answer: B - Explanation: Chaos Engineering involves proactively injecting failures (like killing a server) into a system to ensure the automated recovery mechanisms actually work.
Q54. Which practice maximizes the number of successful service and product changes by ensuring that risks have been properly assessed? (A) Change Enablement (B) Incident Management (C) Service Desk (D) Problem Management
Answer: A - Explanation: Change Enablement (formerly Change Management) aims to maximize successful changes while protecting the live environment from adverse impacts.
Q55. In modern Agile/ITIL v5 contexts, who should typically approve a 'Standard Change'? (A) The Change Advisory Board (CAB) (B) The CEO (C) It is pre-authorized and requires no additional approval (D) The customer
Answer: C - Explanation: Standard changes are low-risk, pre-authorized, and well-understood (e.g., a password reset or expanding a standard VM). They do not require CAB approval.
Q56. What is the primary purpose of a Change Advisory Board (CAB) in ITIL v5? (A) To approve every single code commit (B) To act as a bureaucratic bottleneck (C) To assess and authorize high-risk, complex, or unusual changes (Normal Changes) where cross-functional insight is needed (D) To manage daily incidents
Answer: C - Explanation: The CAB should be reserved for significant changes where diverse expertise is needed to assess broad organizational risk, not for routine deployments.
Q57. What is an 'Emergency Change'? (A) A change requested by a VIP (B) A change that must be introduced as soon as possible, like resolving a major incident or applying a critical security patch (C) A standard change (D) A change that failed testing
Answer: B - Explanation: Emergency changes are rushed to fix a critical issue. They bypass some normal testing/approval steps to restore service quickly, but are reviewed post-implementation.
Q58. Which ITIL practice encompasses the planning, design, and construction of new or changed services? (A) Service Design (B) Service Operation (C) Service Strategy (D) Continual Improvement
Answer: A - Explanation: Service Design focuses on deliberately planning the architecture, processes, policies, and documents required to meet business needs.
Q59. What is the Guiding Principle 'Focus on Value' primarily about? (A) Making the most money (B) Ensuring that everything the organization does maps directly or indirectly to creating value for stakeholders (C) Cutting costs at all costs (D) Increasing shareholder dividends only
Answer: B - Explanation: 'Focus on Value' dictates that all activities—processes, tools, projects—must ultimately contribute to the perceived value of the service consumers.
Q60. The Guiding Principle 'Start Where You Are' advises against: (A) Reusing existing processes (B) Starting from scratch and throwing away perfectly good current state capabilities without analyzing them first (C) Measuring the current state (D) Improving existing services
Answer: B - Explanation: 'Start Where You Are' warns against the 'rip and replace' mentality. You should objectively assess the current state and leverage what already works.
Q61. 'Progress Iteratively with Feedback' recommends: (A) Multi-year waterfall projects (B) Organizing work into smaller, manageable sections that can be executed and evaluated in a timely manner (C) Ignoring user complaints during development (D) Delivering everything at once
Answer: B - Explanation: This principle aligns with Agile methodologies, advocating for small, iterative deliveries to adapt to changing requirements and gather continuous feedback.
Q62. What does the principle 'Collaborate and Promote Visibility' combat? (A) Transparency (B) Information silos and hidden agendas (C) Teamwork (D) Open communication
Answer: B - Explanation: Collaboration across boundaries and making work highly visible prevents siloed thinking, builds trust, and ensures everyone works towards the same goal.
Q63. 'Think and Work Holistically' means: (A) Focusing only on your specific department (B) Understanding that no service, practice, or department stands alone; the organization must work as an integrated whole (C) Ignoring the broader business context (D) Working in isolation
Answer: B - Explanation: A holistic approach ensures that all four dimensions of service management (Organizations & People, Information & Technology, Partners & Suppliers, Value Streams & Processes) are considered together.
Q64. 'Keep it Simple and Practical' advocates for: (A) Creating complex, bureaucratic processes to cover every edge case (B) Eliminating any process, service, action, or metric that fails to provide value or produce a useful outcome (C) Writing 500-page manuals (D) Adding unnecessary steps for security
Answer: B - Explanation: This principle is about lean thinking—cutting out waste and ensuring processes use the minimum number of steps necessary to achieve the objective.
Q65. 'Optimize and Automate' indicates that organizations should: (A) Automate broken processes immediately (B) Rely primarily on human intervention for speed (C) Maximize the value of human work by standardizing and optimizing a process first, then automating it to eliminate toil (D) Avoid automation
Answer: C - Explanation: Optimization must precede automation. Automating a bad process just does the wrong thing faster.
Q66. What are the 'Four Dimensions of Service Management' in ITIL? (A) Plan, Do, Check, Act (B) Strategy, Design, Transition, Operation (C) Organizations and People; Information and Technology; Partners and Suppliers; Value Streams and Processes (D) Costs, Risks, Outcomes, Outputs
Answer: C - Explanation: These four dimensions represent the holistic components needed to effectively manage services. Neglecting any one dimension leads to suboptimal services.
Q67. In the Four Dimensions, what does 'Organizations and People' encompass? (A) Just the HR department (B) Roles, responsibilities, culture, organizational structure, and required skills (C) Server hardware (D) Vendor contracts
Answer: B - Explanation: This dimension focuses on the human element, ensuring the organization has the right culture, structure, and skilled personnel to deliver value.
Q68. Which dimension considers the impact of cloud computing, AI, and databases on service delivery? (A) Organizations and People (B) Information and Technology (C) Partners and Suppliers (D) Value Streams and Processes
Answer: B - Explanation: 'Information and Technology' covers the tools, infrastructure, data, and technological architectures used to support service management.
Q69. What is the focus of the 'Partners and Suppliers' dimension? (A) Competitor analysis (B) The organization's relationships with other organizations that are involved in the design, development, deployment, delivery, support and/or continual improvement of services (C) Internal payroll (D) User training
Answer: B - Explanation: Every organization relies on external partners (like cloud providers, hardware vendors) for service delivery. This dimension manages those relationships.
Q70. What does the 'Value Streams and Processes' dimension focus on? (A) The physical layout of the office (B) How the various parts of the organization work in an integrated and coordinated way to enable value creation (C) The specific brands of routers used (D) Employee salaries
Answer: B - Explanation: This dimension defines the activities, workflows, controls, and procedures needed to achieve agreed objectives and deliver value.
Q71. What is the 'Service Value System' (SVS) in ITIL? (A) A specific software tool (B) A model representing how all the components and activities of an organization work together to facilitate value creation (C) A financial ledger (D) A type of server
Answer: B - Explanation: The SVS is the core architecture of ITIL, showing how Opportunity/Demand feeds into the system and results in Value.
Q72. What are the core components of the ITIL Service Value System (SVS)? (A) Hardware, Software, Network, Data (B) Guiding Principles, Governance, Service Value Chain, Practices, Continual Improvement (C) Strategy, Design, Transition, Operation, Improvement (D) Incidents, Problems, Changes, Releases
Answer: B - Explanation: These five components make up the SVS, providing a flexible framework for managing services.
Q73. What is the 'Service Value Chain' (SVC)? (A) A supply chain for hardware (B) An operating model which outlines the key activities required to respond to demand and facilitate value realization (C) A list of vendors (D) A bureaucratic hierarchy
Answer: B - Explanation: The SVC sits at the heart of the SVS. It consists of six interconnected activities (Plan, Improve, Engage, Design & Transition, Obtain/Build, Deliver & Support).
Q74. Which Service Value Chain activity ensures a shared understanding of the vision, current status, and improvement direction for all four dimensions? (A) Plan (B) Engage (C) Obtain/build (D) Deliver and support
Answer: A - Explanation: The 'Plan' activity provides strategic direction and ensures alignment across the organization.
Q75. Which Service Value Chain activity provides a good understanding of stakeholder needs, continual engagement, and transparency? (A) Improve (B) Engage (C) Design and transition (D) Obtain/build
Answer: B - Explanation: The 'Engage' activity is the gateway for interacting with all stakeholders, including users, customers, and suppliers.
Q76. Which practice is responsible for moving new or changed hardware, software, documentation, processes, or any other component to live environments? (A) Release Management (B) Deployment Management (C) Incident Management (D) Change Enablement
Answer: B - Explanation: Deployment Management is the technical execution of moving components into production. (Release Management decides *when* to make them available to users).
Q77. What is the purpose of the 'Continual Improvement' practice? (A) To align the organization's practices and services with changing business needs through the ongoing improvement of products, services, and practices (B) To increase prices continuously (C) To write more code (D) To hire more staff
Answer: A - Explanation: Continual Improvement ensures that services and management methods stay effective as the business environment changes.
Q78. What does the Continual Improvement Register (CIR) serve as? (A) A list of fired employees (B) A database or structured document used to track and manage improvement ideas securely from identification through to final action (C) A list of software bugs only (D) A financial ledger
Answer: B - Explanation: The CIR offers a transparent, central repository for logging, prioritizing, and managing improvement initiatives.
Q79. Which practice ensures that services achieve agreed and expected performance levels, satisfying current and future demand in a cost-effective way? (A) Capacity and Performance Management (B) Service Level Management (C) Availability Management (D) Financial Management
Answer: A - Explanation: This practice balances the cost of resources against the required performance and capacity needed by the business.
Q80. In Capacity and Performance Management, what does 'Performance' typically refer to? (A) How much storage space is left (B) The measure of what is achieved or delivered by a system, person, team, practice, or service (e.g., response time) (C) The visual look of a website (D) The number of employees
Answer: B - Explanation: Performance often involves metrics like latency, throughput, and transaction response times.
Q81. What is the primary focus of 'Information Security Management'? (A) Buying antivirus software (B) Protecting the information needed by the organization to conduct its business (C) Locking doors physically (D) Ensuring servers never reboot
Answer: B - Explanation: It protects data across the pillars of Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA triad).
Q82. Which practice manages the full lifecycle of all IT assets? (A) IT Asset Management (ITAM) (B) Service Configuration Management (C) Deployment Management (D) Supplier Management
Answer: A - Explanation: ITAM focuses on costs, risks, compliance, and value realization across the lifecycle of software, hardware, and cloud assets.
Q83. How does IT Asset Management (ITAM) differ from Service Configuration Management (SCM)? (A) They are the same thing (B) ITAM manages financial/compliance aspects of components, while SCM manages the technical relationships and dependencies mapping between components (C) ITAM is only for hardware; SCM is for software (D) SCM is outdated
Answer: B - Explanation: ITAM tracks costs and licenses. SCM tracks how a server connects to a database to deliver a specific service.
Q84. What is a Configuration Item (CI)? (A) Any component that needs to be managed in order to deliver an IT service (B) A brand of server (C) A line of code (D) A customer
Answer: A - Explanation: A CI can be hardware, software, documentation, a network component, or even an SLA document.
Q85. Where is data about Configuration Items and their relationships stored? (A) In a spreadsheet on someone's desktop (B) In the Configuration Management System (CMS) / Configuration Management Database (CMDB) (C) In the HR system (D) In the Continual Improvement Register
Answer: B - Explanation: The CMS/CMDB is the authoritative source of truth for the technical topology and relationships of IT infrastructure.
Q86. What is the purpose of 'Monitoring and Event Management'? (A) To randomly check computers (B) To systematically observe services and service components, and record and report selected changes of state identified as events (C) To plan office parties (D) To track employee hours
Answer: B - Explanation: This practice detects occurrences (events) that have significance for the management of the infrastructure or delivery of an IT service.
Q87. In Event Management, what is an 'Informational Event'? (A) A critical failure (B) An event that does not require any action and does not represent an exception (e.g., a user logging in successfully) (C) A warning that a disk is 99% full (D) An event that requires immediate emergency change
Answer: B - Explanation: Informational events are just logged for historical data and auditing; they don't trigger incident workflows.
Q88. What characterizes a 'Warning Event'? (A) Complete system outage (B) An event that allows action to be taken before the business experiences an exception (e.g., CPU hitting 85%) (C) Normal operation (D) A user request
Answer: B - Explanation: Warnings provide a threshold breach alert so proactive action can be taken before a failure (Incident) occurs.
Q89. What is an 'Exception Event'? (A) Normal operation (B) An event that indicates a service or component is operating abnormally or has failed entirely (C) A successful backup (D) A scheduled reboot
Answer: B - Explanation: Exception events typically signify an infrastructure or service failure and automatically trigger the creation of an Incident.
Q90. Which practice is responsible for setting clear business-based targets for service levels, and ensuring delivery against these targets is assessed and managed? (A) Service Level Management (B) Service Desk (C) Relationship Management (D) Incident Management
Answer: A - Explanation: Service Level Management (SLM) defines the SLAs and XLAs in partnership with the business to ensure expectations meet reality.
Q91. What is the 'Watermelon Effect' in Service Level Management? (A) An SLA that is red on the outside (failing metrics) but green on the inside (users are happy) (B) An SLA that is green on the outside (all IT metrics show 99.9% uptime) but red on the inside (the users are completely frustrated and cannot work) (C) A new data visualization tool (D) Measuring fruit sales
Answer: B - Explanation: The watermelon effect occurs when IT hits all their technical targets, but the actual business outcome or user experience is terrible.
Q92. What is the primary purpose of the 'Service Desk' practice? (A) To capture demand for incident resolution and service requests (B) To write software (C) To manage vendor contracts (D) To design new services
Answer: A - Explanation: The Service Desk serves as the single point of contact between the service provider and the users.
Q93. How should the Service Desk be viewed within an organization? (A) As a low-level, unskilled call center (B) As a highly empathetic, vital communicative link that profoundly impacts user perception and satisfaction (C) As an unnecessary expense (D) As purely an automated chatbot with no human fallback
Answer: B - Explanation: Often, the Service Desk is the only part of IT a user interacts with. Their experience heavily dictates their perception of IT as a whole.
Q94. What is a 'Service Request'? (A) An interruption to an IT service (B) A request from a user or a user's authorized representative that initiates a service action which has been agreed as a normal part of service delivery (C) A request to build a completely new application (D) A request to change the company logo
Answer: B - Explanation: Service Requests are pre-defined, standardized, and routine (e.g., requesting standard software, resetting a password, or requesting hardware replacement).
Q95. How does 'Service Request Management' differ from 'Incident Management'? (A) They run on the same platform so they are identical (B) Incident Management handles unplanned interruptions (broken things); Service Request Management handles planned, routine requests for standard services (normal things) (C) Incident management handles standard things; Service Request handles broken things (D) Service requests only deal with hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: Incidents are 'fix what is broken'. Service requests are 'give me this standard thing'.
Q96. Which ITIL practice minimizes the probability and impact of incidents by analyzing incident trends and identifying root causes? (A) Problem Management (B) Incident Management (C) Service Desk (D) Event Management
Answer: A - Explanation: While Incident Management focuses on restoring service quickly, Problem Management focuses on preventing the incident from ever happening again by finding the root cause.
Q97. What is a 'Problem' in ITIL terminology? (A) An unplanned interruption to a service (B) A cause, or potential cause, of one or more incidents (C) An angry user (D) A hardware failure that has been fixed
Answer: B - Explanation: A problem is the underlying root cause. You may have 50 incidents (users can't log in), all stemming from 1 problem (the authentication server is misconfigured).
Q98. What is a 'Known Error'? (A) A user who always makes mistakes (B) A problem that has been analyzed but has not been resolved (C) A bug that will never be fixed (D) A generic error message
Answer: B - Explanation: A Known Error is a problem where the root cause is known, and typically a workaround has been identified, but a permanent fix has not yet been applied.
Q99. What are the three phases of Problem Management? (A) Plan, Do, Check (B) Problem Identification, Problem Control, Error Control (C) React, Analyze, Fix (D) Incident, Request, Change
Answer: B - Explanation: Identification finds the problem. Problem Control analyzes the root cause. Error Control manages known errors and workarounds until a permanent fix is implemented.
Q100. In ITIL v5, what is the role of 'Workarounds' in Problem Management? (A) They are discouraged entirely (B) They are temporary solutions that reduce or eliminate the impact of an incident or problem for which a full resolution is not yet available (C) They are permanent fixes (D) They only apply to hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: Workarounds are crucial for restoring service quickly (supporting Incident Management) while the longer-term structural fix is being developed.
