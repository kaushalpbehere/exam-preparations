ITIL v5 Foundation

LAST-MINUTE CRAM (ITIL v5): Remember the focus on AI integration, automated service management, value streams, digital transformation, and advanced ITSM capabilities.

Set 4 (Questions 151-200)

Q151. Which practice is the responsibility of everyone in the organization? (A) Service Level Management (B) Continual Improvement (C) Problem Management (D) Change Enablement
Answer: B - Explanation: Continual improvement is everyone's responsibility. It is not limited to a specific team or department.
Q152. What is a 'Metric'? (A) A unit of measurement for money (B) A measurement or calculation that is monitored or reported for management and improvement (C) A type of server (D) A customer complaint
Answer: B - Explanation: Metrics are used to understand the current state, measure progress, and identify areas for improvement.
Q153. What is a 'Key Performance Indicator' (KPI)? (A) An unimportant metric (B) An important metric used to evaluate the success in meeting an objective (C) A financial budget (D) A software license
Answer: B - Explanation: KPIs are the most critical metrics that directly indicate progress towards a specific, strategic objective.
Q154. What is a 'Critical Success Factor' (CSF)? (A) A factor that guarantees a project will fail (B) A necessary precondition for the achievement of intended results (C) A type of metric (D) A customer request
Answer: B - Explanation: CSFs are the vital elements that must be in place for a service, process, or project to succeed.
Q155. Which of the following is an example of a CSF for an e-commerce website? (A) 99.99% uptime (B) Number of daily visitors (C) Providing a secure, frictionless checkout experience (D) $10,000 in sales per hour
Answer: C - Explanation: A CSF is a qualitative description of what must happen (frictionless checkout). The KPI would measure it (cart abandonment rate). The Metric would just be the raw data (number of clicks).
Q156. In the context of 'Focus on Value', who ultimately defines what constitutes 'value'? (A) The IT Department (B) The CEO (C) The Service Consumer (D) The software vendor
Answer: C - Explanation: Value is highly subjective. It is always determined by the consumer based on how well the service helps them achieve their specific outcomes.
Q157. What is 'Customer Experience' (CX)? (A) The physical design of a retail store (B) The sum of all interactions a customer has with a service provider (C) The price of a service (D) The speed of a website
Answer: B - Explanation: CX encompasses every touchpoint, from the initial sales process to interacting with the service desk, directly impacting perceived value.
Q158. What is 'User Experience' (UX)? (A) The experience a person has when they interact with a specific product or service, particularly regarding its ease of use and efficiency (B) The cost of licensing software (C) The physical location of a user (D) How fast a server boots up
Answer: A - Explanation: UX focuses on the specific interaction with the product itself (e.g., is the app intuitive?), whereas CX looks at the entire relationship with the provider.
Q159. What does the guiding principle 'Start Where You Are' primarily aim to prevent? (A) Innovation (B) Waste associated with discarding valuable existing capabilities and starting from scratch unnecessarily (C) Collaboration (D) Automated testing
Answer: B - Explanation: The 'rip and replace' approach often creates massive waste. It's usually better to refactor and improve existing systems if they still hold underlying value.
Q160. How should an organization assess its 'current state' before starting an improvement initiative? (A) By asking a single manager for their opinion (B) By relying on outdated documentation (C) By objectively observing the process, measuring it directly, and engaging with the people doing the work (D) By guessing
Answer: C - Explanation: Objective measurement and direct observation (Go and See / Gemba walk) are critical because documentation often differs from reality.
Q161. In an Agile context, what is a 'Minimum Viable Product' (MVP)? (A) A perfectly finished product (B) A version of a product with just enough features to be usable by early customers who can then provide feedback for future product development (C) The cheapest hardware possible (D) A product that only one person uses
Answer: B - Explanation: MVPs are central to the 'Progress iteratively with feedback' principle, allowing organizations to validate assumptions quickly without massive upfront investment.
Q162. How does a 'feedback loop' work in the context of Service Management? (A) It is a loud noise from a microphone (B) It is a situation where part of the output of an activity is used for new input (C) It means managers talk to each other (D) It is an annual review process
Answer: B - Explanation: Feedback loops (like post-incident reviews or Sprint Retrospectives) capture lessons learned to continuously refine operations.
Q163. What happens when an organization fails to 'Collaborate and Promote Visibility'? (A) They save time (B) They create silos, leading to duplicated effort, competing priorities, and hidden risks (C) They increase security (D) They reduce costs
Answer: B - Explanation: Silos are one of the biggest blockers to Agility and Value creation. Visualizing work (like using Kanban boards) builds trust and alignment.
Q164. What is the fundamental idea behind 'Holistic Thinking'? (A) Focusing intently on just the code (B) Understanding that an organization is a dynamic, complex system where altering one part inevitably impacts other parts (C) Hiring holistic medicine practitioners (D) Only thinking about the future
Answer: B - Explanation: A change to technology (Information & Tech dimension) will likely require changes to processes (Value Streams) and training for staff (Organizations & People). You must think holistically.
Q165. When applying the 'Keep it Simple and Practical' principle, what should you do with a process step that provides no value? (A) Automate it (B) Document it thoroughly (C) Eliminate it completely (D) Assign it to an intern
Answer: C - Explanation: If it provides no value to the customer or the business, it is pure waste. Do not optimize it; stop doing it.
Q166. What differentiates an 'Agile' approach from a traditional 'Waterfall' approach in IT management? (A) Agile is slower (B) Agile relies on strict, up-front planning for the entire project (C) Agile focuses on iterative development, cross-functional collaboration, and the ability to adapt to changing requirements mid-project (D) Agile does not use computers
Answer: C - Explanation: ITIL v5 is designed to mesh perfectly with Agile, emphasizing flexibility and continuous, small deliveries rather than massive, risky deployments.
Q167. What is the core philosophy of 'DevOps'? (A) Having Developers and Operations teams sit in different buildings (B) A cultural and professional movement that stresses communication, collaboration, integration, and automation to improve the flow of work between software developers and IT operations professionals (C) Using only one programming language (D) Deleting all documentation
Answer: B - Explanation: DevOps aims to break down the traditional 'wall of confusion' between dev (who want to change things rapidly) and ops (who want maximum stability).
Q168. How does DevOps view the relationship between speed and stability? (A) They are mutually exclusive; you can only pick one (B) They are synergistic; practices that enable speed (like automated testing and CI/CD) actually increase stability (C) Speed is irrelevant (D) Stability is impossible
Answer: B - Explanation: Historically they were viewed in opposition (the wall of confusion). DevOps proved that robust automation allows teams to deploy 100 times a day *more* safely than deploying once a year.
Q169. What is a 'Value Stream'? (A) A river of money (B) A series of steps an organization undertakes to create and deliver products and services to consumers (C) A type of server farm (D) A customer's budget
Answer: B - Explanation: Value Streams map the end-to-end flow of work from the specific trigger (Demand) to the final delivery (Value).
Q170. If a user raises an Incident, what is the 'Trigger' for the related Value Stream? (A) A manager's approval (B) The user experiencing an interruption to their service (Demand) (C) Writing code (D) A server crash
Answer: B - Explanation: The trigger is the initial event—the user needing help. The value stream encompasses everything from ticket creation to final resolution and user satisfaction.
Q171. Why is defining a Value Stream useful? (A) To create pretty diagrams (B) It allows organizations to see the holistic journey of a request, making it easier to identify bottlenecks, non-value-adding steps, and areas for automation (C) To confuse new employees (D) To satisfy auditors
Answer: B - Explanation: You cannot optimize what you cannot see. Mapping the stream makes the entire flow visible so it can be improved.
Q172. What acts as the 'Operating Model' within the overarching ITIL Service Value System? (A) The Four Dimensions (B) The Guiding Principles (C) The Service Value Chain (D) Continual Improvement
Answer: C - Explanation: The Service Value Chain provides the actual, practical step-by-step operating model (Plan, Improve, Engage, etc.) that executes the strategy.
Q173. What is a 'Practice' in ITIL v5? (A) Rehearsing a play (B) A set of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective (C) A strict set of inflexible rules (D) A piece of hardware
Answer: B - Explanation: ITIL shifted from 'Processes' (v3) to 'Practices' (v4/v5) to emphasize that achieving an objective requires more than just a workflow; it requires people, skills, tools, and suppliers (the 4 dimensions).
Q174. Which ITIL practice bridges the gap between the business and technology by managing the investment portfolio? (A) Release Management (B) Portfolio Management (C) Service Desk (D) Event Management
Answer: B - Explanation: Portfolio management evaluates proposed projects against the strategic goals and available funds, deciding which initiatives to greenlight.
Q175. What is the fundamental purpose of 'Organizational Change Management'? (A) To change code safely (B) To manage the human aspects of a transition, ensuring people are prepared, supported, and able to transition to a new way of working (C) To change servers (D) To manage the company budget
Answer: B - Explanation: Projects often fail not because the technology is bad, but because the people refuse to adopt the new tool. OCM addresses resistance to change.
Q176. Which ITIL practice is used to minimize the negative impact of failed changes? (A) Risk Management (B) Problem Management (C) Change Enablement (via remediation planning) (D) Asset Management
Answer: C - Explanation: Part of authorizing a change in Change Enablement is ensuring a valid rollback/remediation plan exists in case it fails.
Q177. What is 'Risk' defined as in ITIL? (A) A guaranteed failure (B) A possible event that could cause harm or loss, or make it more difficult to achieve objectives (Can also be defined as uncertainty of outcome) (C) A certainty (D) A known bug
Answer: B - Explanation: Risk is essentially the probability of something happening multiplied by the impact if it does happen. It represents uncertainty.
Q178. What is the goal of 'Information Security Management'? (A) To restrict all access to everything (B) To align IT security with business security and ensure that the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the organization's assets, information, data, and IT services match the agreed needs of the business (C) To buy firewalls (D) To monitor employees
Answer: B - Explanation: Security must be balanced. Too much security destroys user productivity; too little destroys the business.
Q179. What does 'Confidentiality' mean in the CIA triad? (A) Data is accurate (B) Data is accessible when needed (C) Ensuring that information is observed by or disclosed to only those who have authorized access to it (D) Data is encrypted
Answer: C - Explanation: Confidentiality prevents unauthorized disclosure (e.g., patient records are only seen by authorized doctors).
Q180. What does 'Integrity' mean in the CIA triad? (A) Data is locked (B) Ensuring the completeness and accuracy of information and protecting it against unauthorized modification (C) Data is available (D) Data is cheap to store
Answer: B - Explanation: Integrity ensures the data hasn't been maliciously or accidentally altered (e.g., a bank balance isn't changed by a hacker).
Q181. What does 'Availability' mean in the CIA triad? (A) The system looks nice (B) Ensuring that information and vital services are accessible and usable when required by an authorized entity (C) Data is hidden (D) The system is 100% bug-free
Answer: B - Explanation: Availability ensures the system is actually up and running when the users need it (the core focus of most IT operations).
Q182. Which practice coordinates the resolution of 'Major Incidents'? (A) Service Desk (B) Incident Management (specifically Major Incident Management procedures) (C) Problem Management (D) Service Level Management
Answer: B - Explanation: Incident Management has specialized workflows for Major Incidents due to their high urgency and impact requiring rapid coordination.
Q183. Who usually owns a 'Problem' once it is identified? (A) The Service Desk Analyst who logged the first ticket (B) A Problem Manager or designated subject matter expert (SME) who leads the root cause analysis (C) The CEO (D) The user who reported it
Answer: B - Explanation: Problem investigation requires time and technical expertise, making it a separate role from the front-line Service Desk analysts.
Q184. What is a 'Workaround'? (A) A permanent solution (B) A temporary way to restore service, reducing the impact of a problem until a permanent fix is deployed (e.g., rebooting a server to clear a memory leak) (C) Deleting a file (D) Ignoring the user
Answer: B - Explanation: Workarounds are vital for keeping the business running while developers work on the underlying bug.
Q185. How does 'Problem Management' interact with 'Change Enablement'? (A) They do not interact (B) Problem Management often creates a Request for Change (RFC) to implement the permanent structural fix that resolves the Known Error (C) Change Enablement creates problems (D) They are the identical practice
Answer: B - Explanation: Once Problem Management finds the root cause (e.g., a bug in the code), they must use the Change process to safely deploy the patched code into production.
Q186. Which ITIL practice sets the expectations for IT performance by dealing with XLAs and SLAs? (A) Financial Management (B) Service Level Management (C) Capacity Management (D) Incident Management
Answer: B - Explanation: SLM acts as the bridge defining the agreed-upon targets between the business consumers and the IT providers.
Q187. What is the fundamental shift from SLAs to XLAs? (A) Changing the font on the document (B) Shifting from measuring output metrics (like 'server uptime is 99.9%') to measuring outcome metrics (like 'users are efficiently able to process orders without frustration') (C) Making the agreements longer (D) Firing all relationship managers
Answer: B - Explanation: Experience Level Agreements focus heavily on user sentiment and actual business enablement rather than isolated technical statistics.
Q188. Why might an IT organization appear successful based on SLAs, but fail based on XLAs (The Watermelon Effect)? (A) Because users don't understand IT (B) Because the technical metrics chosen for the SLA do not accurately reflect the critical factors influencing the user's ability to achieve their goals (C) Because SLAs are magic (D) Because XLAs are too complex
Answer: B - Explanation: A network might be 'up', but if the application latency is so high that it takes 5 minutes to load a page, the user's experience is terrible despite the passing SLA.
Q189. What is a key activity of 'Relationship Management'? (A) Repairing laptops (B) Understanding stakeholder needs, managing expectations, and maintaining a high level of satisfaction between the service provider and consumers (C) Writing code (D) Managing network switches
Answer: B - Explanation: Relationship managers are the strategic liaisons, ensuring IT understands the business strategy and the business understands IT capabilities.
Q190. Why is 'Service Desk' considered a highly critical practice despite often handling lower-level technical issues? (A) Because they are cheap to run (B) Because it is the primary interface between the IT organization and its users; a poor Service Desk experience implies poor IT overall regardless of actual technical capability (C) Because they fix servers (D) Because they write software
Answer: B - Explanation: Perception is reality. A brilliant engineering team with a rude or unresponsive Service Desk will be viewed as a failed IT department by the business.
Q191. How is 'Omnichannel' support relevant to modern Service Desks? (A) It means only using email (B) It provides users with multiple interconnected, seamless ways to contact support (phone, chat, portal, social media, walk-in) based on their preference and urgency (C) It means no support at all (D) It uses only pigeon carriers
Answer: B - Explanation: Modern users expect to interact with IT through various channels just as they do with consumer services.
Q192. What is paramount for a Service Desk agent to possess? (A) A PhD in Computer Science (B) Strong empathy, excellent communication skills, and a customer-service mindset (C) Ability to write Assembly code (D) Physical strength
Answer: B - Explanation: Technical skills can be trained. Empathy and communication are critical for de-escalating frustrated users and accurately capturing the impact of an incident.
Q193. How do 'Service Requests' differ from 'Incidents' regarding authorization? (A) Incidents require CEO approval; Service Requests do not (B) Service requests are often pre-approved as standard changes; Incidents are unplanned and handled immediately to restore service (C) They are identical (D) Only managers can raise Service Requests
Answer: B - Explanation: A request for Microsoft Office (Service Request) is a standard, planned procedure. A broken email server (Incident) requires immediate troubleshooting.
Q194. What does the 'Service Catalogue' provide? (A) A list of all hardware in the building (B) A consumer-facing list of all active IT service offerings available for request, detailing descriptions, costs, and SLAs (C) A database of errors (D) A list of fired employees
Answer: B - Explanation: The Service Catalogue functions like a menu at a restaurant, showing users exactly what they can order from IT and what to expect.
Q195. Which Service Value Chain activity is heavily reliant on the Service Desk interacting directly with users? (A) Plan (B) Engage (C) Design (D) Build
Answer: B - Explanation: The Service Desk is the primary operational embodiment of the 'Engage' activity, connecting consumers to the value chain.
Q196. How does ITIL v5 define 'Outcomes'? (A) Physical servers (B) A result for a stakeholder enabled by one or more outputs (The actual business goal achieved) (C) A piece of software (D) A printed report
Answer: B - Explanation: A report is an 'Output'. The 'Outcome' is that the business used that report to increase sales by 10%.
Q197. Why does ITIL focus on Outcomes rather than Outputs? (A) Because outputs are too easy to measure (B) Because delivering an output (a server) that doesn't enable a desired outcome (a working application) creates no value (C) Because outcomes are cheaper (D) They focus on outputs
Answer: B - Explanation: Value is tied to outcomes. IT risks irrelevance if it focuses merely on delivering technical outputs without ensuring they solve the business problem.
Q198. What is the fundamental concept behind a 'Service'? (A) Selling hardware (B) Enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks (C) Writing a contract (D) Managing a data center
Answer: B - Explanation: By providing an email 'Service', IT takes on the cost/risk of buying servers and managing spam, allowing the business to simply achieve its outcome: communication.
Q199. What are the three components of a 'Service Offering'? (A) Hardware, Software, Network (B) Goods, Access to Resources, Service Actions (C) Plan, Build, Run (D) Incidents, Problems, Changes
Answer: B - Explanation: A service offering might give you a laptop (Goods), a VPN login (Access), and a promise to fix it if it breaks (Service Action).
Q200. Give an example of 'Access to Resources' in a Service Offering. (A) Handing the user a physical mobile phone (oops, that's Goods) (B) Providing a user with access credentials to an enterprise CRM software hosted in the cloud, allowing them to use it without owning the underlying servers (C) A technician installing RAM (D) A training manual
Answer: B - Explanation: Access to resources allows the consumer to utilize a capability (like a cloud database or a wifi network) that is owned and managed by the provider.
